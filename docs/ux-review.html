QVAC Music Desk · test/37 · 9 September 2026

A capability map, not yet an instrument

A review of the Music Desk UI against one goal: someone who has never used it, and is not a musician, should get a good song out of it quickly. Every claim below is either read out of the installed addon, measured on this machine today, or quoted from the model authors. Your own example brief is worked end to end and rendered both ways in part two.

reviewed public/index.html, app.js, server.js addon installed @qvac/audiogen-ggml 0.3.3 app documented for 0.3.2 machine arm64, 18 cores, 39 GB ACE-Step DiT on disk turbo-q4 only

Built, on 9 September 2026. This review is now a record of why.

All eight steps of part three shipped: the three doors on one sheet, the brief expander with its two code-side guards, verbs on takes with a draggable waveform range, batches of seeds, the cover-strength table unlocked, the DCW defaults corrected, capabilities read from the addon at startup, and per-engine tag translation. Two bugs were found while building it and are recorded in the session log: a queue race that marked a healthy render failed, and a grid item without min-height:0 that pushed the transport bar off screen.

The desk is an excellent, honest map of what the two engines can do. It is organised around the engines. A music tool has to be organised around what you want to hear.

The single most important finding is not a design opinion. The engine already ships the feature you asked me to invent, and the app does not call it: simpleMode takes one sentence and has the model write the caption, the lyrics, the tempo, the key and the structure itself. It is in the addon that is installed right now, and the ACE-Step authors recommend it as the entry point for exactly the person you are aiming at.

Part one · What is wrong, with the evidence

Twelve findings. Four are hard blockers for a newcomer, three are places where the app now states something about the engine that stopped being true when the addon moved from 0.3.2 to 0.3.3.

1

The first decision it asks for is the one decision a newcomer cannot makeblocker

The top bar makes you choose MiniMax-Music3 or ACE-Step 1.5 before anything else. Neither name means anything to someone who wants a song, and the choice silently removes most of the app: pick MiniMax and Cover and Edit chain go grey.

It is also barely a real choice. Every input-audio feature belongs to one engine, and the speed gap is close to an order of magnitude, so the task decides the engine in every case but one.

Measured, compute time divided by audio length, lower is faster ACE-Step turbo-q4 today: 36.24 s of audio in 10.67 s, so 0.29x. MiniMax measured on this machine on 7 September: 3.99 s of audio in 17.5 s, so 4.37x. On the one task where both work, MiniMax costs roughly fifteen times the wait.
2

The engine already writes the prompt for you, and the app never asks it toblocker

This is the answer to "maybe it makes sense to have an LLM reformulate my request". You do not need to add one for the basic case. ACE-Step's own language-model stage does it, through a flag the app does not send anywhere in server.js, engine/worker.cjs or public/app.js.

With simpleMode: true the caption is a short natural-language query and the LM composes the complete request before synthesis: a detailed caption, full lyrics, and every metadata field you left unset (BPM, key/scale, time signature, vocal language, and duration when duration is 0). @qvac/audiogen-ggml 0.3.3 README, added in 0.3.2 (1 September 2026)
Verified today, one sentence in, nothing else set Query: a sad piano song about a breakup, female voice. Result: 36.24 s of audio, 10.67 s of compute, peak -0.9 dBFS, RMS -16.9 dBFS, per-5s RMS -22, -15.7, -14.6, -14.9, -18.2, so a spread of 7.40 dB. For comparison, our own earlier measurements on the same rig: a hand-written structure-tag prompt reached 4.74 dB of spread and a caption-only take 0.18 to 1.05 dB, which is one loop. One take each, so treat the direction as the finding and not the exact figure: the model's own songwriter arranged the track better than our best hand-authored prompt.
Start with Simple Mode, then move to Custom Mode once they understand how the system responds. ACE-Step 1.5, musicians guide, on the recommended path for newcomers
3

Nothing gets you started, and the main button is disabled until you doblocker

On first load the transport reads idle, write a style caption and Render is disabled. The empty state asks a non-musician for the one thing they do not know how to write. There is no preset, no example, no "make me something", and the Takes panel is empty with no suggestion of what a take even looks like.

The 51 style chips underneath are the closest thing to help, and they are a flat wall: five groups, every chip the same weight, appended to the free-text box. They are a vocabulary, not a path. A newcomer does not know that cumbia and tape saturation are different kinds of decision.

4

The words on screen are the model's, not the listener'sblocker

Reading the interface top to bottom: the caption the model conditions on, Frames instead, 25 per second, Haar DCW correction, LM top-p, LM guidance, Reinforce these hints inside the conditioning caption, Locked to 1, cover-nofsq is the only cover task implemented, Choice of DiT, Frozen semantic codes.

Roughly forty per cent of the width is a left rail holding three panels that cannot make a sound: file paths with sizes in GB, a sixteen-row capability matrix annotated ACE-Step only and MiniMax only, and the library. That matrix is documentation of the SDK. It is genuinely useful, and it belongs in the README it already appears in, not on the working surface.

5

The capability matrix is a hard-coded literal, and it has already gone stalenow wrong

var CAPS in public/app.js is a hand-written table of what each engine accepts, and package.json depends on ^0.3.2, so 0.3.3 installed itself and the UI did not notice. Four controls the engine now has are absent, and one constraint the UI presents as a fact is no longer one.

The locked control is the one with published musical meaning app.js:610 sets opts.audioCoverStrength = 1 under the comment "The addon rejects anything but 1 until context switching lands", and the UI prints Locked to 1. The 0.3.3 changelog opens with: "Support the full audioCoverStrength range for cover-nofsq. Values below 1 follow the source for that fraction of the diffusion run and finish freely afterwards; 0.5 starts as a cover and diverges halfway." Meanwhile the ACE-Step authors publish a usage table for that exact dial: 0.3 to 0.5 for dramatic genre changes, 0.5 to 0.7 for moderate, 0.7 to 0.9 for subtle. So the desk disables the only cover control that has documented musical meaning, and explains the lock with a sentence that is no longer true.

Fix the symptom by unlocking it. Fix the cause by reading the capability set from the addon at run time, so a semver-compatible bump can never make the interface lie again.

6

The DCW sliders sit at twenty times the official strengthtrap

dcwEnabled defaults to true in the engine, with official scalers of 0.05 and 0.02. In the Advanced panel the checkbox reads "enabled" and is unchecked, and both sliders sit at 1.00.

The good news, from reading buildBody(): unchecked sends nothing, so the default path is correct and nobody has been getting a wrong render by accident. The trap is what happens when a curious person ticks the box that says "enabled". They do not enable the correction, they enable it at twenty times and fifty times its official strength. A control whose labelled default is not the engine's default is worse than no control.

7

Making the model invisible needs a translation layer, and there is noneblocker

This is the part of "the user should not know which model runs" that is easy to miss. The two engines do not read the same structure tags, and the app has one shared Lyrics box with one palette of ten lowercase tags that matches neither published list exactly.

Note the collisions: [Pre-Chorus] against [Pre Chorus], [Instrumental] against [Inst], [Build] against [Build Up]. [Drop], [Breakdown] and [Fade Out] appear only in ACE-Step's list; [Hook], [Post Chorus], [Transition] and [Break] only in MiniMax's. If the desk is going to route between engines on the user's behalf, it has to rewrite the section markers on the way through, and today it forwards them unchanged.

One caveat I could not remove MiniMax's published limits (style prompt 10 to 300 characters, lyrics 10 to 3000) come from the hosted Music API, not from this addon. I checked: index.js:668 enforces only "caption must be a non-empty string", with no length rule for either engine. So treat 300 characters as what the model was served with rather than a local hard limit, and clamp toward it rather than asserting it.
8

The app offers a control the model authors tell you not to use

The checkbox "Reinforce these hints inside the conditioning caption" sends augmentCaptionWithMetadata, which the addon documents as appending BPM, time signature and key to the internal conditioning caption.

Avoid: BPM, key, or tempo data in the caption field. ACE-Step 1.5, official songwriting prompting rules, under Caption Format & Rules

These may not be the same path: one is a user typing "128 bpm" into the caption, the other is the engine assembling its own conditioning string. I have not measured it, so I am not calling it a bug. I am saying a control that does the one thing the authors list under critical do-nots should be measured before it stays in a consumer surface, and it certainly should not be presented as "reinforce", which reads like an improvement.

9

One take per click, where the authors say two to four

The desk renders a single take and its repaint lives inside an expert Edit chain, a montage table with start and end fields in seconds that you fill in before you have heard anything. Both of the authors' workflow rules point the other way.

Always generate 2-4 versions at once. Fix, Don't Redo: use Repaint rather than regenerating entirely. ACE-Step 1.5, musicians guide, workflow

At 0.29x on turbo-q4, three variations of a 30 second idea cost about 26 seconds of compute in total. Picking from three is a completely different experience from judging one, especially for someone with no vocabulary to describe what they would change.

10

The length default is set for a sound effect, not a song

The Length slider defaults to 24 s over a 4 to 240 range. For anything with vocals that is far too short, and the authors are specific about why.

2 verses + 2 choruses: 120-150s minimum. Plus a bridge: 180-240s. Full production with intro and outro: 210-270s. When in doubt, estimate longer. A song too short feels rushed. ACE-Step 1.5, duration calculation

They also give the lyric budget that goes with it: about 90 to 140 words for a 47 second track, and 6 to 10 syllables per line. None of that guidance is anywhere in the interface, and the Lyrics box is an empty textarea with a two-line placeholder.

11

Export is a multi-select listbox of thirteen formats

A four-row <select multiple> holding wav, flac, alac, aiff, caf, m4a, aac, opus, ogg, ac3, wma, mp2, pcm. Multi-select listboxes are the least discoverable control on the web, and nobody making a song for a video needs alac and mp2. The one format a normal person will look for is mp3, which the vendored ffmpeg cannot write. Say that once, offer wav and m4a, and put the rest behind Advanced.

12

Two features are unreachable on this machine and the UI does not say so

Only turbo-q4 is in ~/.qvac/models. Flow-Edit is turbo-only so it works, but lego stems require the base DiT, and the addon's own README says "The base DiT is not in the registry ditVariant set yet", so it has to be supplied as an explicit path. The desk's resolver only knows the three registry variants, which means the stem feature cannot be reached from this app at all today, even though the engine has it. Worth stating in the interface as a missing file with an action, the way the MiniMax folder already is.

Part two · What I would build instead

Keep every ACE-Step capability. Change what the screen is organised around: from which engine and which parameter to describe it, hear it, fix it.

Two doors, one song sheet.

There is a brief box for the person who wants to talk about their game, and there are the fields for the person who already knows they want 76 BPM in A minor. Both write the same object, and the sheet is what renders. So the brief box is not a beginner mode with a ceiling, and the fields are not an expert mode you graduate into. The brief fills the sheet, you read what it decided, and you overrule any line of it.

That single object is what stops this becoming two products. A wizard that hides the fields has to grow its own controls forever. A form with a "help me write this" button leaves the newcomer staring at the form. Making the sheet the shared artefact means the newcomer sees, in the words the model actually uses, what their sentence turned into, which is the only way they ever learn what "fingerpicked" or [building energy] changes.

Organised now

  • Engine first, in the top bar, by model name
  • Mode second: Compose, Cover, Edit chain
  • Then a form of every parameter the engine accepts
  • Left rail: what the machine has, and a capability matrix
  • Right rail: takes, as a log
  • Render is one button at the bottom, disabled by default

Organised instead

  • One prompt field and a Make button that is never disabled
  • Starting points as cards that fill the field with a working sentence
  • The song sheet the model composed, shown and editable
  • Takes are the centre, each with verbs: longer, change this part, restyle, cover, stem
  • Engine chosen by the task, named only in the status line, after the fact
  • Everything else behind one Advanced door, at the engine's real defaults

Read the spread honestly 14.4 dB against 5.4 dB is not a quality verdict. The tracks are different lengths, and a longer track has more blocks and therefore more chance of variation. Looking at the actual block sequence, Simple Mode's figure is mostly its intro ramp (-25.6, -19.8, then about -13 for the body) plus one dip to -19.9 around two minutes. Both takes are arranged rather than looped, which is the thing worth knowing. What is not a judgement call is that the raw brief produced three minutes when nobody asked for three minutes.

The other thing this run settles: 0.12x. Three minutes of music in twenty-two seconds. That is fast enough for the whole design above to feel like an instrument rather than a render queue, and fast enough that generating three variations per click costs nothing worth protecting the user from.

You asked whether an LLM should reformulate the request, and you added that it must not be the only way in. That makes three routes, not two. All three write the same sheet, so none of them is a separate product.

Why Simple Mode cannot be the only route: it overrides the length you ask for Measured today, same query and seed, only the flag changing. Plain call, asked 8 s, produced 7.2 s. So: Surprise me is Simple Mode, one button, promising a song and not a length. Describe it is the default, because a visible sheet is what turns a newcomer into someone who understands the tool. Write it is the same sheet with an empty brief box, which is what you use when you already know you want 76 BPM in A minor and do not want anything reinterpreting that.

What the expert door has to guarantee

This is the half that is easy to lose when a tool gets friendlier, so it is worth stating as requirements rather than as a promise.

  • Nothing rewrites a field you touched. Once a field is yours it is yours, including on a re-render, including when you change the brief. Mark it and never silently recompute it.
  • The brief box is optional and stays empty. No modal, no wizard step, no "are you sure you want the advanced view". The sheet is the screen.
  • Every parameter the engine accepts is reachable. That is what today's app already does well, and it must survive the redesign. Behind one Advanced door, but reachable, at the engine's real defaults.
  • The raw caption goes through untouched. Someone who wants to type a comma-separated tag list, which ACE-Step also supports, gets exactly their string sent.
  • The seed stays visible. Reproducibility is the expert's whole workflow and the current app is already right about this.

The expander's system prompt, built from the authors' own rules

These are not my preferences. Every line comes from the ACE-Step prompting rules or the musicians guide, so the expander teaches the model's documented conventions rather than my guesses. 

# Caption
Prose, not a comma list. A short paragraph describing the whole song's vibe.
15 to 25 words. 5 to 12 specific keywords; past 15 they dilute each other.
Specific beats vague: "grand piano" over "piano", "fingerpicked acoustic
guitar" over "guitar". Cover style, emotion, instruments, texture, era,
vocal character. NEVER put BPM, key or tempo in the caption.
No conflicting descriptors unless framed in time ("starts soft, turns intense").

# Lyrics

when it plans the arrangement.
Sections: [Intro] [Verse] [Pre-Chorus] [Chorus] [Bridge] [Build] [Drop]
          [Breakdown] [Guitar Solo] [Piano Interlude] [Outro] [Fade Out]
Voice:    [whispered] [falsetto] [powerful belting] [harmonies] [ad-lib]
Energy:   [high energy] [building energy] [explosive]

Two things this buys beyond convenience. It lets the app fill the Lyrics field with real structure, which is the single measured quality lever we know of on this engine. And it gives the user a sheet they can argue with, which is how they learn what "fingerpicked" changes.
